About Tenzor

Tenzor is a list of students from the Faculty of Mathematics and Physics, continuing the work of the earlier list Vektor. We run in FMF's Student Council and ŠOUL elections.

STUDENT COUNCIL CANDIDATES

Timen Bobnar

Computer Science and Mathematics (IŠRM)

Running for a seat on the Student Council at the Faculty of Mathematics and Physics. Having completed the practical mathematics track and now continuing at master's level in computer science and mathematics, and with more than two years' experience as a tutor, I understand the challenges students face as well as their needs. I want more connection between students through events and activities that enrich the study experience and encourage collaboration. Students should have a real voice in the decisions that shape our study environment.

Aleksej Luka Golobič

Financial Mathematics

I'm a student of financial mathematics running for the ŠOUL assembly. I play music, read philosophy, and play sports. I decided to run because, since my first year, it's been clear to me that ŠOUL has become a distorted version of the organisation meant to represent students' interests — one that mostly preserves the current, poor state of affairs, where any attempt at change gives way to the self-interest of the ruling group. ŠOUL could instead be a tool for broader student organising that genuinely improves student life. As a member of the assembly I would push for:

  • Better scholarship and housing policy
  • Better, cheaper, less commercial parties
  • More social extracurricular activities
  • Interdisciplinary lectures and round tables
  • Greater transparency in how ŠOU operates
TENZOR MEMBERS

Lara Pustinek Miočić

Mathematics

I first encountered university student organisation when I started at Radio Študent — also the only outlet that actively covers student politics, which is where I learned most of what I know about it. I'm running because I want to move beyond watching and take a more active part in student political decision-making, and to stand up for students' rights generally and at FMF, where I'm in my second year of mathematics, in particular.

Maj Požar

Physics

Now in my third year of general physics. I know student hardships — from housing to otherwise — both from my own circle and from activist work. I believe that running for election can improve students' social standing and political awareness, and get us better included in decisions about what support students actually need. I'm standing on the Tenzor list as a representative for culture and publishing.

Staša Korpič

Physics

In my second year of physics, meteorology track. Living and study conditions for students keep getting worse — higher food prices, inaccessible housing in Ljubljana, a shortage of study spaces, poor student jobs. Many of these problems look unsolvable at first glance, but I think building a strong, unified student organisation that works for students' benefit is the first step toward solving them. At the faculty I'll push for more accessible student events that build connection between students and give room for sharing ideas and finding solutions.

Jakob Sever Klasinc

Mathematics

I study mathematics and I'm running for the FMF student organisation on the Tenzor list. I've been active before in Maribor and Ljubljana, helping organise events, workshops, and rallies around environmental protection and workers' rights. I think FMF students have a chance to organise more formally and achieve real, material change — and that we not only can but should raise awareness of the problems students face, and their broader place in things.
