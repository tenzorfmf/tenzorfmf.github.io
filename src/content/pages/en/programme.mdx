## Elections

This year, on Wednesday 9 October, the elections of the Student Organisation of the University of Ljubljana (ŠOU) will take place. ŠOU is a democratically organised association of students that represents students' interests and strives to realise them.

## Why should the elections matter to us?

The student organisation has nothing to do with students, except that: **ALL OF US STUDENTS MAKE UP THE STUDENT ORGANISATION. WE STUDENTS FUND ŠOU. FROM EVERY HOUR OF WORK A STUDENT DOES THROUGH A STUDENT WORK REFERRAL, 3.8% OF THE EARNINGS IS TAKEN, AND PART OF IT GOES TO THE STUDENT ORGANISATION. ŠOU COUNCIL MEMBERS MANAGE 4,990,000 EUROS OF STUDENT MONEY EVERY YEAR[^1]. THEY SPEND THIS MONEY AS IF IT WERE THEIR OWN, SO THE AVERAGE STUDENT GETS ALMOST NOTHING FROM THE ORGANISATION'S WORK[^2]. THERE IS NO END TO ŠOU'S CONTROVERSIAL PRACTICES. FROM PRIVATE PARTIES[^3] ON THE CAMPUS INVESTMENT PROPERTY[^4] TO PUBLIC STUDENT MONEY FLOWING INTO THE PRIVATE HANDS OF COUNCIL MEMBERS' FRIENDS[^5]. YET WE HEAR LESS AND LESS ABOUT IT, BECAUSE THEY ACTIVELY OBSTRUCT RADIO ŠTUDENT'S REPORTING[^6]. WORST OF ALL, THE ORGANISATION DOES NOT DEAL AT ALL WITH THE PROBLEMS STUDENTS FACE. INSTEAD OF SOLVING THE HOUSING CRISIS AND DISCUSSING THE WORSENING MATERIAL CONDITIONS OF STUDY, THEY OFFER US EVEN MORE PARTIES AND SPORTS EVENTS. BECAUSE OF THEIR OWN INTERESTS WITHIN ŠOU, ELECTED STUDENT REPRESENTATIVES AVOID ACTIVE PARTICIPATION IN DEMOCRATIC PROCESSES, WHICH PRESERVES THE STATUS QUO. THIS LETS THE ŠOU DIRECTOR RUN THE ORGANISATION LIKE A PRIVATE COMPANY FOR ALMOST A DECADE, WHILE RECEIVING A GROSS SALARY OF 5,250[^7] EUR, PAID OUT AS A PERFORMANCE BONUS.**

Elections take place every two years, so the next time you notice the student organisation's problems, your voice will not be heard, because new generations will once again be wondering what student politics has to do with them.

## What can we change?

In the elections, FMF students elect representatives at two levels:

- two members of the **Student Council** (ŠZ),
- four representatives of the **Student Organisation of the Faculty of Mathematics and Physics** (ŠO FMF).

### Student Council

The Student Council is the highest legislative body of the Student Organisation of the University of Ljubljana. It is made up of elected representatives of the UL member faculties. In the Student Council, the two members vote on how ŠOU's budget is spent on solving study-related problems, student welfare and health care, help for foreign students, and organising sports, cultural and social events.

### Student Organisation of FMF

ŠO FMF representatives are responsible for running extracurricular programmes at FMF. Their funding is provided by the Student Council. Their purpose is to connect the students of the faculty where they operate: workshops, round tables, trips and social events, such as FMF's traditional maths-physics picnic.

Through these bodies we students can decide how our student money is spent, improve student life at the faculty, connect, cooperate and create better conditions for study together. Would you like a ping-pong table in the student room? We can get you one!

## Election programme for the Student Council

### Democratisation and transparency of ŠOU

Not knowing about the Student Organisation is not a phenomenon limited to FMF. Turnout at the last ŠOU elections was 6% across the university. Student politics therefore lacks new faces and ideas, and ŠOU's vision is drifting away from students' actual hardships. That is why we will push for more active awareness-raising among students about ŠOU's role and work. Under the current arrangement, the Student Council itself appoints the presidency, the electoral commission, the appeals bodies and other bodies. There is practically no external oversight, and after the elections we students have no way of influencing the Student Council's work. We will advocate greater democratisation of student representative bodies. ŠOU's funds are provided by UL students, so it is in our interest that they are spent sensibly and effectively.

### Student work and income

"I'm a student so that I can study!" Studying without worrying about survival is sadly no longer a reality for many of us. Overburdened students work an average of 25 hours a week on top of their studies, eleven hours more than the European average. Two fifths of students do regular paid work during the lecture period[^8]. Student work is supposed to give students practical knowledge and experience in their field of study, but in practice it substitutes for the lack of adequate economic support that should make studying possible in the first place.

We demand better public infrastructure and a scholarship policy that allows students to devote themselves fully to their studies. We stand for removing private intermediaries from student work (student employment agencies) — their role should be taken over by the Ministry of Labour, and the 3.8 % commission that currently goes into intermediaries' pockets should instead go towards raising the hourly rate. We must establish working additional forms of solidarity and social assistance for students in need. The current forms of direct financial aid are insulting and useless. In 2024 the Student Organisation's institute, Študentski tolar, offered 75 students a one-off payment of 200 EUR and 10 student families 500 EUR[^9]. These are minimal amounts when we recall that the Court of Audit reported that between 1 January 2020 and 30 June 2022 ŠOU recorded entertainment expenses (hospitality for student officials) totalling 43,847 EUR.

### Housing policy

Ljubljana does not have adequate capacity to house all students. There is not enough room in the dorms, rents are too high and living conditions are poor in both cases. This housing situation is nothing new but the result of decades of neglect by the responsible institutions. So we do not expect to turn it around immediately. But the student organisation has extensive financial resources that we can immediately put towards starting the renovation of old dorms, as well as real estate that can likewise be used to ease the crisis. In the long term, we commit to using our position in ŠOU to push the state and the municipality to take action and find solutions to students' housing problems.

### Reading rooms

Reading rooms are places where students have a quiet space to do their coursework, often with access to the study literature they need. Reading rooms are the only places that allow students without a room or flat of their own to study quietly and effectively and to meet their study obligations. Because access to reading rooms is linked to academic success, differences in students' material circumstances only deepen further, especially given that scholarships depend on academic performance. Here too we will use our position in ŠOU to put pressure on the university, the municipalities and state bodies, and to work with libraries (especially CTK) to find solutions.

## Election programme for ŠO FMF

### Social programme and connection

FMF brings together students from all walks of life, united by shared interests. So many new faces can be intimidating for many, and apart from parties and drinking, too few social events are currently organised at FMF where students could make real connections and widen their social circle through shared interests. That is why we stand for broadening the range and frequency of events at FMF, such as board-game tournaments, D&D campaigns and anything else that encourages socialising that does not depend on alcohol – without, of course, abolishing the more party-oriented events.

### Solidarity programme

A fund for financial aid to students. Study materials, such as textbooks and books of solved problems, are crucial for successful study. But libraries often have too few of them to meet the needs of all students, who are therefore forced to buy their own materials, which is too great a financial burden for many. In light of this, we aim to set up a system for lending these materials through a textbook fund, to reduce the financial burden on students. The textbooks would come from buy-backs and donations from students who no longer need them, and from purchasing new materials with funds available to the student organisation.

### Mafia picnic

We aim to move the well-known mafia picnic to a location better suited to the event.

We share the view of many senior students that the picnic has lost much of its charm because it is held on the student campus – a concrete playground with a few square metres of grass. With our list's candidacy, we promise to explore potential alternative venues, taking students' wishes into account.

### Academic engagement

We will organise guest lectures and professionally relevant excursions to other scientific and research institutions (CERN, SISSA, DESY, LNGS …). We will organise student interest groups at FMF and try to revive the FMF magazine (https://revija.fmf.si). For the well-read, and those who would like to become so, we plan to use round tables to bring current topics from study and non-study fields closer to students and to give them a chance to express their views on them.

### Hygiene products

Pads and tampons of various sizes must be available at all times in all FMF restrooms. We will stock the restrooms with free hygiene products for anyone who needs them.
